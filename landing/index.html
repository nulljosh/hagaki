葉書
はがき · hagaki

Japanese for postcard. 葉 is leaf. 書 is write.

One theory says the word comes from the tarayō tree. Scratch its leaf with a stick and the writing shows up dark, so people wrote on leaves.

Every Japanese postcard has seven red boxes for the postal code. They are there so machines can sort the mail. Hagaki does the same for your inbox. Seven boxes, and every letter goes in one.

Seven boxes

Receipts, travel, dev, newsletters, social, promotions and junk. Each one is a real label in Gmail or a real folder in iCloud, under a folder called Hagaki.

Nothing is deleted

Filing moves mail out of your inbox and into its box. Junk gets a box too. Delete is a separate button, and in Gmail it goes to Trash, not the void.

People stay put

Mail from a real person, with no unsubscribe link and nothing bulk about it, stays in your inbox where you will see it.

Look first

The list is a preview. Nothing moves until you press File everything. Unsubscribe is one click and sends the sender's own one-click request where they support it.
